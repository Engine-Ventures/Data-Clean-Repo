How much of the index reached the pipeline?

Every company folder under New Deals / 02. Companies — the whole index, one row each — against the two systems that record a live deal: Affinity and the new deal slides. A folder in both is a company EV worked and tracked. A folder in neither is storage carried for something that never entered the pipeline under that name, which is the half worth reading. The join is on name, because the index carries no domain or entity id, so each hit shows what it matched and how.

Trends over 43 weeks

Four questions: how the funnel narrows, how the stage mix moved week to week, when new companies entered, and how much of the record is actually filled in. Every count carries its denominator.

Funnel

Companies that got at least this far, and the share of the previous rung that converted. Reached, not observed — a company can skip a rung in a week, so the two differ by design. Preliminary Diligence is the base rung: this file is screened to companies that reached it, so there is no wider population to convert from here.

Stage mix, week by week

One column per meeting, in true date order; segment height is the number of companies sitting in that stage that week. The four hatched columns are weeks with no slide — an absent meeting is not a quiet week. A company appears in a week only if it sat in a diligence stage that week, so a column is shorter than the meeting's full slide.

The trace — position over 43 weeks

One lane per company, one step per meeting. Height and colour both carry the stage — Preliminary Diligence sits on the floor, Legal at the ceiling — so a climb reads as a climb. A ring means the name was bold that week. A dashed connector spans meetings where the company was not listed at all, because its position then was never observed. This chart keeps the categorical stage colours of the deck; the charts above use an ordered ramp.
